#### 2026-09-09: the extra instruction is a spill-versus-colour decision

The `+4` is not in the body. The target keeps `arg0` across the
`camGetRotationMtx` call by spilling it to its own incoming argument home
(`sw a0, 0x58(sp)` in the guard's delay slot, `lw t0, 0x58(sp)` after the
call) -- two instructions. The candidate colours it into `s0`, which costs
three: the save, the `move`, and the restore. Everything else about the entry
block already agrees.

What decides it is measured: **IDO spills an unmodified parameter to its
incoming home and colours a modified one.** Removing `arg0 *= 4` in favour of
`(arg0 * 4)` as an expression moves the candidate straight onto the target's
`sw a0, home` / `lw reg, home` pair, drops `s0` entirely, and puts `sw ra` back
at `0x14`. That is the first half of the answer and it is reproducible in one
build.

It costs the other half. Without the write-back the product is a single CSE
value and `sortOffset` coalesces into it, so the target's `or t0, t7, zero`
copy disappears and the candidate lands at 163 words, and the frame grows from
`0x58` to `0x60`. Five spellings of that statement group were measured -- the
expression twice, offset-then-object, object-then-offset, a `<<` / `*` mix, an
indexed `&array[arg0]`, and a separate `byteOffset` local -- and every one of
them is 163 words with no copy. So the two halves are coupled the way
overlay99's two levers were, and the next attempt has to supply the copy from
a source form that still leaves `arg0` unmodified.

Also flat in this pass, so do not repeat: `difference = arg1 - arg0` as one
statement, an extra declared local (used or unused), removing `remainder`, and
inlining the `matrix` pointer (that one costs nine words).
